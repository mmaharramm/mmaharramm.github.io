## Overview

My overall experience with the exam was pretty smooth. However, what helped me the most was my previous knowledge and experience in penetration testing, which proved useful for the majority of the questions.

I would recommend this certification to people who are genuinely interested in getting into threat hunting **with Falcon** and already have some experience, particularly in penetration testing or red teaming.

If this is your first certification exam, or at least your first CrowdStrike exam, make sure to take notes and actively participate in the instructor-led training (ILT). Also, follow the official CCFH exam guide: read the recommended documentation, complete the learning materials, and take notes along the way.

Use AI to practice exam questions and simulate threat hunting scenarios. Use CQL extensively, and learn how to interpret events by studying the Events and Data Dictionary documentation provided by CrowdStrike. You should be in a good position to pass!

## Why I Took It

My main goal was to make threat hunting with CrowdStrike second nature, as I perform threat hunting activities quite frequently.

Although I already had some experience with threat hunting through Elastic SIEM labs, which provide a relatively straightforward approach compared to Falcon, I wanted to improve my skills and become more proficient with the Falcon platform.

So far, the journey has been going pretty well!

## My Background Going In

Before taking this exam, I had already obtained two certifications:

- **eJPT** – Junior Penetration Tester by INE Security.
- **CJCA** – Certified Junior Cybersecurity Associate by Hack The Box (definitely not junior :D).

I already had a good understanding of both offensive and defensive security methodologies, which helped me significantly during the exam, even though these certifications and their learning materials are external to CrowdStrike.

Additionally, I had spent around eight months working with the Falcon platform before taking the exam.

## Preparation

As you probably know, this certification consists of a multiple-choice exam, and you only get to practice some threat hunting activities during the ILT training.

For someone with little to no experience with Falcon, I would recommend the following:

- Read the official exam guide thoroughly.
- Create a checklist to track your progress and make sure you cover all the exam objectives.
- Complete all the recommended learning materials.
- Take notes throughout your preparation.

If you feel that you are missing some knowledge, consider taking the Incident Responder ILT training as supplementary material.

If you want to increase your chances of passing the exam, I would also recommend using AI to simulate hands-on labs and generate mock exam questions for additional practice.

Another important aspect is understanding penetration testing methodologies. Complete a couple of Linux and Windows labs, learn how real attackers operate, and familiarize yourself with the procedures and commands they use at each stage of an attack.

**Hunt more!** Proactively hunt for different threats using Falcon. This will help you gain more hands-on experience with:

- Writing CQL queries.
- Navigating different sections of the Falcon platform.
- Pivoting across indicators.
- Investigating and handling different leads.
- Understanding attacker behavior and identifying suspicious activity.

If you apply these recommendations, I believe you will have a great chance of passing the exam!

### Resources, Labs & Practice

Some of the most useful knowledge I gained came from Hack The Box (HTB) Academy. Combined with the CCFH training, these resources provided a really good learning experience.

You can complete the following modules to build a beginner-to-intermediate understanding of security methodologies and techniques. You can then apply that knowledge both during the exam and in your daily work.

Here are the modules I recommend:

- [Windows Fundamentals by HTB](https://academy.hackthebox.com/app/module/49)
- [Linux Fundamentals by HTB](https://academy.hackthebox.com/app/module/18)
- [Windows Command Line by HTB](https://academy.hackthebox.com/app/module/167) – **Very important!**
- [Intro to Penetration Testing by HTB](https://academy.hackthebox.com/app/module/295)
- [Incident Handling Process by HTB](https://academy.hackthebox.com/app/module/148)
- [Windows Event Logs by HTB](https://academy.hackthebox.com/app/module/216) – **Extremely important!**
- [Security Monitoring & SIEM by HTB](https://academy.hackthebox.com/app/module/211)
- [Threat Hunting by HTB](https://academy.hackthebox.com/app/module/214) – **A great addition to your knowledge base!**

## Exam Day

Since I was already well-prepared and my daily tasks complemented my threat hunting knowledge, I decided to take the exam during my break, and I passed!

Don't overcomplicate things or assume that the exam is extremely difficult. If you understand the methodologies and technical concepts I mentioned above, you should be in a good position to pass.

Aim for that 60/60 score!

## Was It Worth It?

If you are planning a career in threat hunting with Falcon, I believe this certification is worth pursuing. Even if you intend to use another product for threat hunting, the ILT training materials provide valuable knowledge that you can apply regardless of the platform.

However, this recommendation mainly applies to people who can obtain training and exam vouchers through their organization.

If you have to pay for both the training and the exam voucher out of your own pocket, it might not be the best investment. In that case, I would recommend focusing on practical certifications and hands-on learning opportunities, which could be much more beneficial for developing your technical skills.

If that's your situation, consider going for **CJCA or CDSA**, or completing their respective learning paths instead.
